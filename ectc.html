Method and limitations

This report uses metadata for Electronic Components and Technology Conference papers from 2016 to 2025 indexed by OpenAlex (CC0); coverage depends on its indexing. Front matter such as title pages and session overviews is removed. Phrases are word sequences taken from titles and abstracts; OpenAlex has no full text. A paper counts when the phrase is in its title or appears more than once in its abstract after acronym normalization. This is a relevance heuristic, not a topic label: repeated background or application mentions can still count. Acronyms the corpus defines (“redistribution layer (RDL)”) are read as the same phrase. Fragments that almost always sit inside a longer phrase (“level packaging”) are folded into it, and the lists show one phrase per family. 7 phrases from a reviewed boilerplate list (high-performance, next-generation, low-cost, cost-effective, wide range, high-quality…) are left out of rankings when used in three or more other fields. Cross-field technical phrases, such as deep learning, remain eligible. “Rising” and “fading” compare the pooled share of the first half of the period with the second.

Topics come from non-negative matrix factorisation of TF-IDF vectors with a fixed seed, so they are reproducible; labels are the topic's top terms. Institution names and parent relationships are OpenAlex's, which are good but not perfect.
